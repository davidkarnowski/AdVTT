### S11. Adblock Radio, "Designing an audio adblocker for radio and podcasts" (2018-11-15) — https://www.adblockradio.com/blog/2018/11/15/designing-audio-ad-block-radio-podcast/index.html

- Relevance to AdVTT: the one shipped acoustic ad blocker concluded host-read ads defeat texture classification; its repetition/fingerprint idea remains valid for produced spots.

### S19. Reddy et al. (Spotify), "Detecting Extraneous Content in Podcasts" (EACL 2021; arXiv 2103.02585) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.02585
- Type: paper
- Verified: fetched (PDF saved by earlier run, read locally)
- Key facts: "extraneous content" = sponsor ads, cross-promos, host self-promotion. Gold transcript set: 506 episodes, 62.25 % contain EC; 12,918 sentences, 24.01 % EC. ### S21. IAB Tech Lab, "Podcast Technical Measurement Guidelines v2.3 — Draft for Public Comment" (released 2026-07-21, comments to 2026-08-21) — https://iabtechlab.com/standards/podcast-measurement-guidelines/ (PDF saved by earlier run)
- Type: spec
- Verified: fetched (local PDF)
- Key facts: §4.3 "Dynamically Inserted Ads": ads "dynamically inserted at the time of" request; for streamed consumption ads "may be inserted into a file that is being progressively" delivered. Byte-range requests are first-class: apps commonly send a 2-byte probe (Range: 0-1) then range requests; 206 partial responses are counted under rules; measurement via URL-prefix redirect chains (multiple prefixes per URL). Re-fetching the enclosure from a different IP/UA is exactly what measurement guidelines treat as new downloads — an ethics/etiquette note for any differential fetch feature.

### S22. Six, "Olaf: a lightweight, portable audio search system" (JOSS 8(87):5459, 2023-07-03) — https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.05459
- Type: paper/repo
- Verified: fetched (local PDF)
- Key facts: C implementation; spectral-peak fingerprints combining two or three peaks (two-peak: shorter/noisier queries, more collisions; three-peak: fewer false positives); runs on ESP32 (≥250 kB RAM; 20 s query vs 1 h index < 512 kB) and as WebAssembly in browser; Ruby CLI wrapper handles transcoding and "checks for duplicate material"; positioned as baseline for "broadcast audio monitoring" and duplicate detection in datasets. ## Synthesis

**1. Industry structure (Q1).** Delivery and creative type are two different axes, and most public commentary conflates them. Delivery: DAI carried 48 % of US podcast ad revenue in 2019, 67 % (2020), 84 % (2021), 92 % (2022) [S17]; every later source repeats ">90 %" [S5, S17]. Creative: host-read still leads by spend — Magellan AI puts host reads at 46 % of Q3-2025 spend (43.8 % a year earlier) versus 26 % produced spots, with produced spend growing faster (+67 % vs +32 %) [S49]; Podscribe reports programmatic impressions rising from 3 % to 14 % YoY [S47]. A widely repeated 58 %/29 %/8 % (host/programmatic/producer-read) split for 2025 could not be traced to any primary document [S2 correction]. Because host reads are now routinely recorded separately and stitched at request time ("stitched into a marked ad break at the moment the file is requested" [S49]), **"inserted" no longer implies "produced/announcer"** — a DAI splice can bound a host-read ad. Placement by revenue has been stable at roughly 31 % pre / 63–66 % mid / 4–6 % post since 2021 [S17a]; IAB defines pre-roll as "positioned in the first two minutes" [S17]. Spot lengths: about half of spots are 16–30 s and > 40 % are 31–90 s [S17]; hosts accept 15/30/60 s (plus 5/10 s) creatives [S24]. Ad load: 8.0–8.3 % of episode time (Magellan, ~95 k episodes/quarter) [S3, S56] versus 10.1–10.9 % (Podscribe/Oxford Road) [S4, S47] — i.e. 5–6.5 minutes per hour. Spotify's annotated corpus found 62 % of episodes contain ads/promos and 24 % of sentences in those transcripts are extraneous content [S19]. Revenue: $1.93 B (2023) → $2.4 B (2024) → $2.86 B (2025) [S18, S48, S1].

**2. How DAI works and what it leaves behind (Q2).** All hosts examined (Megaphone, Acast, Omny, Transistor, Podbean; ART19 snippet-only) define insertion points as timestamps of type pre/mid/post [S6, S7, S24–S26]; Megaphone allows up to 15 (5/5/5) and adds "Remove"/"Replace" cuepoints that alter the served audio after publication [S6]; Acast caps midrolls (1 for 10–19 min, 2 for 20+ min), lets "preroll" sit up to 25 % into the file and "postroll" up to 50 % from the end, and offers "snap to silent areas" and automatic back-catalogue markers at "silent points detected by our software" [S7, S8]; Omny auto-places markers by duration rules [S24]. Stitching happens server-side per request, for downloads and streams [S5, S24], producing a complete byte-addressable file that players fetch with 2-byte probes and range requests [S21]. Two things follow: (a) splice points cluster at silences, so silence-gap + loudness-step is a principled boundary cue (also assumed by DirecTV's alignment patent [S23]); (b) two fetches of one episode can differ only in ad regions — the "cross-fetch differential" that MinusPod implements with a 0.60 correlation ceiling and 10 s hold floor [S32, S57], and that **Amazon patented** (two versions + fingerprint alignment + Hunt–McIlroy transcript diff + transformer "starting-word" ad splitter) [S58]. **No public write-up of MP3 frame/header artefacts exists** (gap); no host documents loudness matching of creatives, and practitioners observe "sections mastered at different levels than the content baseline" [S32], so a loudness step is plausible but unquantified.

**3. Fingerprinting repeated ads (Q3).** Mature, local, landmark-style tools return match offsets in both query and reference: Panako (`Query start/stop`, `Match start/stop`, AGPL, JVM) [S34], Olaf (C/Zig, `--fragmented` 30 s chunks, dedup, ESP32/WASM; README warns of Shazam patents US7627477/US6990453) [S22, S35], audfprint (Python, `--find-time-range`) [S40]. Chromaprint is a whole-track identifier [S62] — usable to re-identify a known ad but not to discover repeats. Neural fingerprints (NeuralFP, PeakNetFP) need CUDA/TF stacks and are music-trained [S39]. Repetition-based commercial detection is a two-decade literature (Gauch & Shivadas; Chen & Li MRS; EEUPC) [search log], and Adblock Radio used a "hotlist" of known ads plus ~30 s maximal-repeated-sequence search [S11, S33]. Every acoustic system that met host-read ads concluded the same thing: "Native advertisements, where the regular speaker reads sponsored content" are "a limit of the ad / talk / music classification" [S11]; RadIA and Spotify moved to transcripts for exactly that reason [S10, S19]. Fingerprints therefore cover the produced/programmatic slice (~25–30 % of spend, rising) and platform promos, not the host-read majority.

**4. Local acoustic segmentation (Q4).** `ffmpeg -af ebur128=metadata=1` yields momentary (400 ms) and short-term (3 s) LUFS per frame with no extra dependencies [S27]. Silero VAD is MIT, < 1 ms per 30 ms chunk [S37]. inaSpeechSegmenter is strong for speech/music/noise/noEnergy but explicitly tags "speech over music ... as speech" [S36], so it cannot detect a music bed under a voice-over; YAMNet exposes "Background music", "Theme music", "Jingle (music)" (indices 262–264) [S13, S30], and MusicNet shows 81.3 % TPR at 0.1 % FPR is achievable with a tiny CNN [S54]; Essentia has voice/instrumental only [S59]. AudioSet has **no Advertisement class** [S13]. pyannote is gated behind HF tokens [S38].

**5. Read vs spontaneous prosody (Q5).** Spotify's 2024 study reaches AUC 0.95 (scripted F1 0.83) on 1,930 podcast episodes using Whisper-encoder features, but handcrafted prosodic features collapse cross-domain (AUC 0.53–0.74) and all results are episode-level [S12]; older prosodic work reports 93.7 %/69.5 % [S53]. Segment-level "this 60 s is scripted inside a conversational show" is untested — a genuine research gap and a plausible weak signal since AdVTT already runs Whisper.

**6. Metadata priors (Q6).** JSON chapters v1.2 and ID3 CHAP carry no ad semantic [S14, S52]; ad marking is a title convention promoted by Podnews ("especially with extra context like the coupon code") and hidden via toc=false [S28]; adoption is unquantified (gap). Apple, Spotify and Overcast now auto-chapter, Apple since iOS 26.2 — none ad-aware [S28, S29]. SponsorBlock's database is public and large (36 k+ labelled videos used in one study) [S20, S43], and Spot-SponsorBlock extends it to Spotify [S44], but practitioners report offsets do not transfer between YouTube and RSS audio [S45]. Show notes can be dynamically rewritten per campaign [S25].

**7. Ad-free feeds and contentiousness (Q7).** Apple serves a separately uploaded ad-free file to subscribers [S15]; Supercast and Supporting Cast run ad-free private feeds for shows with 10k–100k paid subscribers and partners like NPR and Wondery [S60, S61]; a transcript-based ad *remover* (Feedcast) is sold on the Mac App Store [S55] and several open-source removers exist [S9, S32, search log]. The industry's own line is "your audience is already skipping ads. So skipping can't be prevented" [S28]. The one behaviour that measurably harms publishers is double-downloading, which "doubles the feed's download count in the publisher's stats" [S32] and is counted as a download under IAB rules [S21].

**Contradictions.** Ad load 8 % vs 11 % (different panels/definitions) [S3 vs S47]; host-read share 46 % (Magellan spend) vs "two-thirds" (older IAB-attributed line) vs unverified 58 % [S49, S2]. The WebFetch summary of the USPTO patent PDF mis-stated the assignee; Google Patents is authoritative [S51 vs S58].

## Implications for AdVTT

1. **Add an acoustic corroboration channel, never a standalone cutter.** Compute per-frame EBU R128 M/S loudness [S27] and Silero VAD silence spans [S37]; at each LLM-proposed boundary, search ±3 s for a silence gap and a loudness step, and (a) snap the edge (targets `boundary_err_sec` ≤ 3 s), (b) raise confidence when both agree, (c) lower it when the proposed span has no acoustic discontinuity at either end. MinusPod's fusion stance — acoustic evidence corroborates/extends, cuts alone only at ≥0.8 [S32, S57] — matches the false-positive asymmetry. Priority 1; ffmpeg-only.

2. **Per-show repeated-segment index for produced spots.** Fingerprint every episode with Olaf `--fragmented` or audfprint `--find-time-range` [S35, S40]; a ≥15 s span that recurs across ≥2 episodes (or matches a confirmed ad) is strong evidence of a produced ad or platform promo — the ~26 % of spend the transcript channel handles worst, and the fastest-growing slice [S49, S47]. Exclude the show's own intro/outro theme (also repeats) by position and by first-seen episode. Check Olaf's LICENSE and the Shazam patent status before bundling [S35]. Priority 2.

3. **Differential download: opt-in, off by default, legal review first.** It is the only hard evidence for DAI slots [S32], but Amazon's US 12,190,871 claims two-version comparison + transcript diff + ML segmentation [S58], it doubles publisher download counts [S32, S21], and it finds *inserted* regions — which increasingly include host-read reads [S49]. If kept, emit provenance `INSERTED` (a delivery fact) distinct from `ADVERTISEMENT` (a content judgment). Priority 3.

4. **Music-bed-under-speech as a "produced" feature.** Run YAMNet and log "Background music"/"Jingle (music)" scores per 0.96 s frame [S30]; do not use inaSpeechSegmenter for this [S36]. If FPR on intros is tolerable, feed the fraction of the span with music-bed into the confidence model. Priority 4; needs the experiment in Open Questions.

5. **Scripted-vs-spontaneous prosody is a research item, not a roadmap item.** Episode-level AUC 0.95 with Whisper-encoder features [S12] suggests a segment-level head trained on AdVTT fixtures + SponsorBlock sponsor reads [S43] could flag host-read reads; nothing published proves it at 30–90 s granularity.

6. **Use existing metadata as priors, never as truth, and record provenance.** Parse ID3 CHAP, podcast:chapters JSON and show-notes timestamp lines [S14, S29, S52]; pass sponsor names from descriptions into the LLM prompt as candidate advertiser labels (they may be campaign-injected [S25]); use SponsorBlock segments only after transcript re-alignment [S45].

7. **Recalibrate expectations and gates with industry numbers.** Expect 5–6.5 min of ads per hour [S3, S47], 1–5 breaks with 1–3 spots of 15–90 s each [S6, S7, S17], pre-roll within the first two minutes [S17]; the ">35 % ad = reject" gate is safe (3× the highest observed load). Expected coverage of non-transcript channels: acoustic boundary cues apply to the ~92 % of ad dollars delivered by DAI [S17]; fingerprints to the ~26–30 % that is produced/programmatic [S49, S47]; host-read baked-in ads (≤8 % of revenue but over-represented in small shows) remain transcript-only.

8. **Output design.** Add acoustic evidence fields to the cue JSON (`loudness_step_lu`, `silence_gap_ms`, `fingerprint_match`, `inserted_by_diff`) and consider also emitting a Podcasting 2.0 chapters JSON with `toc:false` for ad spans [S14, S28] — the one format players already consume. Ground-truth fixtures can come from ad-free subscriber files diffed against public files [S15, S60].

9. **Positioning.** Metadata-only output with auto-skip off is less contentious than shipping removers (Feedcast, MinusPod) that the App Store and GitHub already host [S55, S32]; the industry itself sells ad-free feeds [S15, S60, S61]. Avoid default double-downloads [S32, S21] and cite the Amazon patent in the legal track.

## Open questions / things that need an experiment

1. **Loudness-step statistics.** On the 4 fixtures plus ~20 fresh DAI episodes (Megaphone, ART19, Acast, Omny hosts), measure the M-loudness step and silence gap at true ad boundaries vs random content boundaries; determine whether platforms loudness-match creatives (no host documents it).
2. **MP3-level artefacts.** Inspect stitched files for frame-header changes (bitrate/mode/sample-rate), Xing/LAME/Info header presence, bit-reservoir discontinuities and ID3 differences at splice points; nothing public exists [Gaps].
3. **How often do two fetches differ?** Sample 50 episodes across hosts, fetch twice with different UA/IP, align by fingerprint, and record the fraction with differing regions and their lengths — the empirical coverage of the differential channel.
4. **YAMNet music-bed detector.** Evaluate "Background music"/"Jingle (music)" scores on host-read ads, produced ads, intros and content; report TPR/FPR at 0.96 s and span level.
5. **Segment-level scripted/spontaneous classifier.** Train a small head on Whisper-encoder pooled features over 30 s windows using fixtures + SponsorBlock reads; test whether it separates host-read ad reads from conversation.
6. **Repeat-index false positives.** Quantify how often show themes, recurring segments and cross-promos repeat across episodes so the fingerprint channel's exclusion rules can be set.
7. **Legal.** Claim scope of US 12,190,871 B1 (Amazon) vs a loudness/silence-only or single-file approach; Olaf/Panako licences; Shazam landmark patent expiry.
8. **Metadata coverage.** Measure, on the user's PodcastFetch library, what fraction of feeds carry ID3/JSON chapters, ad-titled chapters, or show-notes timestamp lines (Podcast Index stats were unreachable).
9. **Locate the IAB Oct-2024/Oct-2025 podcast "Part 2" studies** for post-2022 host-read vs announcer-read and DAI shares.
